01 / the case

A broken chain hides the cause.

In the fictional Northstar Energy scenario, fragmented platforms disagree on who the customer is, which premise and meter an account belongs to, and whether a read supports a bill.

THE PAIN

Manual reconciliation

Operators investigate mismatched identifiers, stale reads and conflicting event dates by hand.

THE RISK

Unclear evidence

A bill-to-read link can look present while pointing at the wrong meter or provenance.

THE RESPONSE

Rules + ownership

Profile each source, define controls with business owners and route uncertainty to stewards.

04 / practical AI

AI helps the team see sooner.

The quality gate stays deterministic. AI proposes patterns and priorities; a person decides consequential changes.

01 · SIGNAL

Source drift detection

Rank unusual shifts in nulls, codes, volumes and freshness. Engineer confirms cause.

02 · PRIORITISE

Exception queue

Suggest work order from severity, age and approved impact attributes. Steward can override.

03 · ASSIST

Rule drafting

Draft a rule from an owner definition and synthetic examples. Peer review before execution.

Hard boundaries

  • No inferred consent or missing-read generation
  • No automatic customer merge or bill alteration
  • No real personal data sent to public AI
  • Suggestions are not evidence of correctness

Assurance gates

  • Purpose, minimisation and approved access
  • Privacy, security and model-risk review
  • Shadow comparison, error and drift monitoring
  • Human override, incident route and rollback

06 / interview narrative

Show the judgement, not just the rules.

“I built a synthetic customer-to-bill data quality programme, grounded in public UK guidance and implemented as an Informatica IDMC build specification.”

What makes it credible

  • Names and sample records are explicitly fictional
  • Public sources support context, not supplier allegations
  • Unknown is visible; no invented baseline rates
  • IDMC assets are specified, not claimed as deployed

What to ask the client

  • Which system owns each key and status?
  • What precisely makes a read bill-eligible?
  • What happens when evidence is missing?
  • Which IDMC edition, connectors and runtimes exist?

Sources include Informatica documentation, GOV.UK smart-meter guidance, Ofgem non-domestic billing transparency context and ICO AI/data-protection guidance. The ICO notes that its AI guidance is under review; verify current advice before production.
